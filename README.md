# Getting Started with the Base App

I put this together for anyone who keeps hearing about the Base App and
wants a simple place to start. No hype, no trading tips. Just what the app
is, how to set it up, and the few things you should be careful about before
you put any money in.

If a word here is new to you, check the short glossary near the end.

## First, a quick bit of housekeeping

People use the word "Base" for more than one thing, and that causes most
of the confusion.

Base itself is a blockchain. Think of it as the road. It is an Ethereum
Layer 2, built by the team at Coinbase, and it is where transactions
actually happen. Fees are low and things confirm fast, which is the whole
point of it.

The Base App is the app you install on your phone. Think of it as the car.
It started life as Coinbase Wallet, then it was relaunched as something
bigger than a wallet: a place to post, follow people, trade, pay, and open
other apps, all in one place.

And then there is Base for builders, which is just the docs and tools for
people who make those apps. Most users never need to touch that side.

So: chain = the network, app = what you use, builders = the people making
things for you to use. Keep those apart and the rest makes sense.

## What you can actually do in the app

When you open it, it feels closer to a social app than to an old crypto
wallet. That is on purpose.

There is a feed. It runs on open protocols like Farcaster and Zora, so
posts are not locked inside one company's database the way they are
elsewhere. You follow people, you see what they post, and you also see
what they are trading, which takes a bit of getting used to.

Trading sits right inside that feed. You do not have to go to a separate
exchange, copy an address, and hope you pasted it into the right place.
You see an asset, you can act on it there. That is convenient. It is also
exactly why you should slow down, because it is very easy to tap first
and think later.

You can pay people too, including inside encrypted chats, and you can
open other apps — games, lending apps, little tools — without installing
each one separately.

Creators are a big part of the pitch. Posts can earn when people interact
with them, and some creators have their own creator coin, so their
community shares in how they do. At launch the app also offered a rate on
USDC held in it. Rates like that change, and they are not available
everywhere, so do not take a number from a blog post (or from this guide)
as a promise. Whatever the app shows you today is the only number that
counts.

The app opened to everyone in December 2025, in more than 140 countries.

More detail, straight from Base: https://www.base.org/blog/baseapp

## Setting it up

Go to base.org and follow the download link from there. Please do this
part properly. Do not install a wallet from a link somebody sent you in a
DM, a reply, or a giveaway post. Fake wallet apps are common, and they
look convincing. Typing the address yourself takes ten seconds.

Once it is installed, create a new wallet, or import one if you already
have one.

Then comes the one step people rush and regret. If the app shows you a
recovery phrase, write it on paper, in order, and put the paper somewhere
safe and offline. Not a screenshot. Not a note in your phone. Not an
email to yourself. That phrase is the wallet. Anyone who has it has your
money, and nobody — not Base, not Coinbase, not "support" — will ever
legitimately ask you for it.

Before you move any real amount, spend a few minutes just looking around.
Find your wallet address. Find where it shows which network an asset is
on. Find send, receive, and trade. Find settings and help. It is boring.
Do it anyway.

For your first transaction, use a small amount. Send a little to an
address you control, or receive a little, and watch it land. Look at the
fee. Look at how long it took. Once you have seen one normal transaction,
the next ones stop being scary.

After that, a sensible order to explore in is: read the feed first, then
open a couple of well-known apps and practise connecting and
disconnecting your wallet, then try a small payment to someone you know.
Trading can wait until last.

## Things to be careful about

I will keep this short, because long safety lists tend to get skipped.

Onchain transactions usually cannot be undone. There is no bank to call.
So check the address at the start and at the end before you send, check
that you are on the right network, and use a small test amount with
anything new.

Read what you are signing. If a site asks for an unlimited approval, or
asks you to sign something you do not understand, close it. A real
opportunity will still be there after you have checked. A scam usually
will not wait, and that urgency is the tell.

Ignore support accounts that message you first. Real help is reached from
inside the app and from the official site, nowhere else.

And be suspicious of earnings claims, including friendly ones. Reward
pools end, rates drop, and impersonators copy real announcements word for
word and just change the link.

## If you make content

The interesting change here is that a post is not only a post. When
people trade content in the app, the creator can get a share of the fees
straight to their wallet, without waiting for a monthly payout or hitting
a follower threshold first. There is also a tool, Repurpose.io, for
bringing content over from other platforms automatically.

That said, features change and not everything is live in every country,
so check what your own app actually offers before you plan around it.

## If you build apps

Start at docs.base.org and base.org. Build for the web, test on a real
phone with a real wallet, and use the Base Sepolia testnet before you go
near mainnet. Test the dull parts hardest: connecting, signing,
disconnecting, and what your app does when a transaction fails.

## Questions people ask

Is the Base App just a wallet?

It has a wallet in it, and you do hold and send assets with it, but the
idea was to make it more than that — social, trading, payments, and apps
in one place. That is why it does not look like the wallets from a few
years ago.

Is Base the same as Coinbase?

No. Base is incubated by Coinbase and the app grew out of Coinbase
Wallet, but Base the chain is its own network, and other companies build
on it too.

Do I need crypto already to start?

You need some funds in the wallet before you can do much beyond looking
around. The app offers ways to add funds, and what is available depends
on where you live.

Can I make money just by using it?

There are ways people earn in the app, through content, apps, and rewards.
None of them are guaranteed, and anyone who guarantees you a return is
someone to avoid.

Where do I get help?

From the help section inside the app, or through the official sites
below. Never through an account that contacts you first.

## A few words, plainly

Base — the network everything here runs on.

Base App — the phone app this guide is about.

Feed — the social timeline inside the app.

Creator coin — a coin linked to a creator, held by their community.

In-app browser — the browser inside the app, where other apps open.

Recovery phrase — the list of words that can restore your wallet. Keep
it offline and private.

Basename — a readable name on Base, like yourname.base.eth, instead of a
long address.

USDC — a stablecoin designed to track the US dollar. Common for payments
on Base.
